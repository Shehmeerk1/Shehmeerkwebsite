Most cities grew along a river or a road and filled in later. Phoenix was surveyed first. The land was divided into one-mile squares long before most of it held a house, and the major streets still run along those lines.

## One mile, then another

Drive any direction across the Valley and the big intersections arrive a mile apart. Baseline Road sits on the survey baseline that the rest of the state's land descriptions are measured from. The grid is the reason an address here tells you roughly where you are before you look at a map.

## Why it matters when you buy

The grid shapes daily life more than people expect.

- **Arterials and interiors.** A house one block inside a square lives very differently from one on the mile road itself.
- **Commutes are predictable.** Distance on the grid translates to time fairly honestly.
- **The exceptions are the prizes.** Where a mountain, a canal or a wash interrupts the squares, streets bend, lots get odd, and those pockets tend to be the ones people remember.

When we tour, I start with the map. It saves a lot of driving.
